# Commercial Wall-Art A/B Test Plan - 2026-05-02

## Goal

Test whether one K4 wall-art page can gain broader commercial visibility by using a more machine-obvious buyer/category design while preserving Wayne Heim's authored fine-art voice.

This is a controlled test, not a site-wide rewrite.

## Hypothesis

K4's current wall-art pages may be too art-forward or concept-forward for broad commercial terms like:

- western wall art
- western wall decor
- western wall artwork
- western art prints
- western prints
- cowboy wall art
- cowboy artwork
- western cowboy art prints

Competitors may be winning because their pages use simpler category architecture, plainer H1/title language, product-style sections, and exact internal anchors. K4 can test that structure on one page without flattening the whole site.

## Test Page Recommendation

Primary test candidate: `/Western-Wall-Art`

Why this page:

- It already has the correct commercial/buyer role.
- It is broad enough to test multiple wall-art and print-intent phrases.
- It can become the commercial buffer without disturbing the more artistic authority pages.
- It is safer than rewriting `/Western-Fine-Art-Photography`, which carries the main collector voice.

Control pages to leave mostly unchanged during the initial test:

- `/western-artwork`
- `/cowboy-wall-art`
- `/cowboy-art-prints`
- `/vintage-western-art`

These can receive internal links to the test page, but should not all be redesigned at once.

## Redesign Direction

Make `/Western-Wall-Art` look and read more like a commercial category page, while still being K4.

Required page signals:

- H1 includes `Western Wall Art`.
- Title tag includes `Western Wall Art`, `Western Art Prints`, and/or `Cowboy Art Prints`.
- First visible paragraph plainly states that Wayne Heim offers Western wall art and fine art prints for collectors, homes, offices, lodge spaces, and interior design projects.
- Above-the-fold or early section includes links to key commercial subpaths.
- Page includes practical sections about print formats, room placement, collecting, and subject categories.
- Internal links use varied but clear anchors such as `western art prints`, `cowboy wall art`, `vintage western art`, `western artwork`, and `civil war art prints`.

## Proposed Page Structure

1. Hero / category claim
   - H1: `Western Wall Art`
   - Plain commercial opening.
   - Primary CTA to browse prints or galleries.

2. Shop / browse by subject
   - Cowboy wall art
   - Wild West art
   - Western landscape art
   - Civil War art prints
   - Vintage Western art
   - Black and white Western photography

3. Western art prints for rooms and collectors
   - Living rooms
   - Offices
   - Lodges / ranch spaces
   - Hospitality and interior design

4. Why these are not generic Western decor
   - Painterly photography
   - Narrative scenes
   - Collector-grade presentation
   - Authored Wayne Heim style

5. Print / material path
   - Link to print options.
   - Link to Engrained / wood prints if relevant.
   - Mention framed, display, and sizing paths only where supported.

6. Featured commercial links
   - `/western-artwork`
   - `/cowboy-wall-art`
   - `/cowboy-art-prints`
   - `/vintage-western-art`
   - `/wild-west-art`
   - `/Civil-War-Art`
   - `/Western-Wall-Art-for-Interior-Designers`

7. FAQ
   - What is Western wall art?
   - Are these Western art prints or photographs?
   - What makes Wayne Heim's Western wall art different from standard decor?
   - Where does cowboy wall art fit?
   - Are prints available for interior design projects?

## What Not To Do

- Do not redesign every wall-art page at once.
- Do not rewrite current ranking pages that are already working.
- Do not create new near-duplicate pages for every phrase.
- Do not remove painterly/narrative language; add commercial clarity before the deeper language.
- Do not redirect, noindex, or canonicalize sibling pages during the test.

## Measurement Window

Baseline:

- Capture current GSC impressions, clicks, average position, and indexed URL for the target terms.
- Capture Semrush ranking status for the wall-art and print cluster.
- Record current top landing page for each term where available.

Observation:

- Check at 2 weeks for crawl/index changes.
- Check at 4 weeks for early impression movement.
- Check at 6-8 weeks for ranking movement.

Success signals:

- `/Western-Wall-Art` begins receiving impressions for terms it did not previously match.
- Any target term enters top 100, then top 50.
- GSC begins mapping commercial queries to `/Western-Wall-Art` rather than unrelated gallery/image pages.
- Internal traffic moves from `/Western-Wall-Art` to relevant galleries, print options, and contact/inquiry paths.

Failure / rollback signals:

- Existing traffic drops sharply without new impressions.
- Google maps unrelated queries to `/Western-Wall-Art` and weakens stronger pages.
- Engagement becomes worse because the page feels too generic.

## Follow-Up If Test Works

If `/Western-Wall-Art` gains traction, apply the same commercial-category pattern carefully to:

- `/cowboy-wall-art`
- `/cowboy-art-prints`
- `/vintage-western-art`
- `/Western-Wall-Art-for-Interior-Designers`
- `/Modern-Western-Interior-Design-Art`

Each page must retain a distinct job:

- `/Western-Wall-Art` = broad buyer/decor/prints.
- `/cowboy-wall-art` = cowboy display and room/decor intent.
- `/cowboy-art-prints` = print/product intent.
- `/vintage-western-art` = vintage/old west subject and poster/print intent.
- `/Western-Wall-Art-for-Interior-Designers` = specifier/project intent.
- `/Modern-Western-Interior-Design-Art` = modern/contemporary interior design intent.
